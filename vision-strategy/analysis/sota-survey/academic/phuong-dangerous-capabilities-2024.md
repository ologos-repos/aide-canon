## 1. What it is

**"Evaluating Frontier Models for Dangerous Capabilities"** is the Google DeepMind paper that operationalizes *dangerous-capability evaluation* as a pre-deployment safety discipline: a programme of empirical evaluations that probe a frontier model for latent abilities whose elicitation would enable serious harm, run *before* the model is released so the result can gate deployment and safeguard level. The paper introduces a pilot suite across **four dangerous-capability areas** — **persuasion & deception**, **cyber-security**, **self-proliferation**, and **self-reasoning** — and reports the suite against the Gemini 1.0 model family, finding **no strong evidence of dangerous capabilities** in the models evaluated but identifying **"early warning signs."** The contribution is not a single benchmark score but a *methodology*: a reproducible, capability-by-capability evaluation protocol designed to give "early warning" of dangerous capability *before* it crosses a deployment-gating threshold — the empirical engine that frontier-safety governance policies (Responsible Scaling Policy, Preparedness Framework, Frontier Safety Framework) consume to decide *whether and how* to deploy. It establishes the operative sense of **"capability"** that the whole frontier-safety lexicon now shares: **a latent ability whose elicitation enables harmful action, subject to evaluation before deployment.**

**Citation (verified 2026-06-01):** Mary Phuong, Matthew Aitchison, Elliot Catt, Sarah Cogan, Alexandre Kaskasoli, Victoria Krakovna, David Lindner, Matthew Rahtz, Yannis Assael, Sarah Hodkinson, Heidi Howard, Tom Lieberum, Ramana Kumar, Maria Abi Raad, Albert Webson, Lewis Ho, Sharon Lin, Sebastian Farquhar, Marcus Hutter, Grégoire Delétang, Anian Ruoss, Seliem El-Sayed, Sasha Brown, Anca Dragan, Rohin Shah, Allan Dafoe, Toby Shevlane. *Evaluating Frontier Models for Dangerous Capabilities.* arXiv:2403.13793 (v1 20 Mar 2024; v2 5 Apr 2024). **Terminology note — the load-bearing vocabulary collision.** This paper, together with the RSP and the Preparedness Framework, fixes the **frontier-safety sense of "capability": a latent ability whose elicitation enables harmful action, subject to evaluation before deployment.** This **COLLIDES** with the **product-UX sense** of "capability" — *a feature exposed to a user* — which the canon's vocabulary map records as a known collision ([A3] in the vocabulary collision register; cf. [`../../aide-vocabulary-map.md`](../../aide-vocabulary-map.md)). The two senses are nearly antonymic in governance posture: the product-UX "capability" is something you *ship and surface*; the frontier-safety "capability" is something you *evaluate and may withhold*. Within the canon, the AEON **Capability plane** (OAAD) and OAgents capability-composition use "capability" in the *compositional/feature* sense — surface this collision at every read and qualify which sense is meant ("dangerous-capability evaluation" vs. "capability plane / capability tag"). This is the same discipline the slice applies to "agent" and "skill": same word, two altitudes, flag-don't-merge. **Vocab hygiene:** the paper's subjects are *frontier models* (and, in the self-proliferation / self-reasoning areas, models acting autonomously) — the canon does **not** apply the casual "agent" to an AI-under-principal ([ADR-EA-0016](../../../../decisions/ADR-EA-0016-adopt-ai-aide-as-canon-vocabulary.md)); where the paper evaluates a *model-under-no-principal* probing autonomy, that is precisely the *ungoverned* form the **AI-aide** framing exists to bring under a principal — name it a model/AI-aide-under-evaluation, never a bare "agent." No Ologos-ecosystem / NG-AIDE-01 entities are implicated by this finding, and there is no "fleet" reference to disambiguate here.

## 4. Position / relationship + HCAE call + synthesis

**Position: the paper SUPPORTS the foundation tier — it is external, independent, lab-grade corroboration of HCAE's eval-gated, human-in-the-loop thesis, arriving at the same place from the misuse-prevention direction.** This is not "ahead/behind" at the conceptual level; on the *eval-harness slice* the canon is behind (it has no harness — see the Inspect finding), but on the *governance argument* the paper and HCAE are convergent allies.

**HCAE call (load-bearing): dangerous-capability evaluation SUPPORTS HCAE's human-curated / eval-gated case — it does not COMPLICATE or SUPERSEDE it.** The reasoning is direct and specific:

- **HCAE argues** that because AI has structural epistemic limits ([AIDK](../../../../foundation/aidk/)), AI work must be *human-curated* — a human holds the locus of judgment and accountability, and AI output is gated on human curation rather than self-certified.
- **Phuong et al. operationalize exactly that gate at model-release altitude:** a model's latent dangerous capabilities are *empirically evaluated, and the evaluation (curated and acted on by humans / a governance body) gates whether and how the model is deployed.* The human-in-the-loop curation HCAE argues for is precisely what frontier-safety governance operationalizes — the RSP's "Capability Threshold → ASL Standard" mechanism, the Preparedness Framework's tracked-category thresholds, and the DeepMind Frontier Safety Framework are all *human-curated, evidence-gated deployment decisions* with this paper as their empirical substrate. Evaluation-before-deployment **is** human curation made into a release gate.
- It does **not COMPLICATE** HCAE: it adds no premise that human curation is unnecessary or sufficient-without-it; on the contrary, the paper's "early warning signs / no strong evidence *yet*" framing reinforces that the gate must stay human-held and re-run as capability scales. It does **not SUPERSEDE** HCAE: it governs *model release*, a different (upstream) altitude from HCAE's *work-product curation* — they stack rather than replace. Frontier-safety eval gates the *substrate model*; HCAE gates the *AI-aide's work under a principal*. Two gates at two altitudes, same epistemic discipline.

**Synthesis — the canon consumes this as foundation-tier corroboration plus an eval-thread adoption target.** The paper supplies the *methodology* (four-area capability evaluation, early-warning thresholds); the OSS eval harness [Inspect AI](../oss-frameworks/inspect-ai.md) supplies the *engine* (Tasks/Solvers/Scorers, sandboxed execution, Agent Bridge) that frontier labs actually use to run this class of evaluation; **OAgents §6** supplies the *conformance frame* (evidence-by-artifact, three verification levels) that wants exactly such graded capability evidence; and **OrdSA evidence-up** supplies the *authority gradient* the threshold-gated-safeguard pattern instantiates. The canon's distinctive contribution remains the layer *above* the eval: an Inspect-run dangerous-capability score measures *what a model can do*, but it confers neither an authority altitude ([OrdSA](../../../../constructs/ordsa/)) nor a runtime behavioral envelope ([OAgents](../../../../constructs/oagents/)) — those stay AIDE-distinctive, sitting *over* the eval output as governance. This is the same canon-spec ↔ Means-substrate relation the survey records for the eval plane in [`../oss-frameworks/inspect-ai.md`](../oss-frameworks/inspect-ai.md) §4 and the observability plane in [`../vendor-stacks/langchain.md`](../vendor-stacks/langchain.md) §4: the canon governs *over* the measured evidence, it does not get measured *by* it.

*Citation discipline.* Phuong et al. (2024) does not cite any AIDE artifact — it predates the canon's published surface and runs in the frontier-lab safety lineage, not the enterprise-AI-architecture lineage. The relationship is one-directional: the canon cites this paper as external corroboration of HCAE's eval-gated thesis and as the methodology anchor for the eval thread. No reverse-citation (external work citing HCAE/OAgents/OrdSA) is present here to track as a Vision success signal — record it as a lineage *input*, not a citation *win*.

## 5. Objective implication

Two Doerr-style Objective shapes follow:

1. **Defend-and-extend (foundation citation + governance-over-eval altitude).** Frontier-safety eval-gating is HCAE's eval-gated thesis independently corroborated at model-release altitude; the AIDE position is the governance layer that *consumes* capability-evaluation evidence rather than being defined by it. KR shape: add Phuong et al. (2024) as a cited external corroboration in the HCAE foundation reading and in the OAgents conformance §, and ship a documented "capability-evidence feeds OrdSA evidence-up / OAgents verification" mapping that explicitly bounds what a capability eval can and cannot certify (it scores behavior; it confers neither authority altitude nor a runtime envelope) — making the SUPPORTS-not-supersedes call cite-able rather than asserted.
2. **Catch-up by adoption (the eval thread).** The canon has no harness to *generate or grade* capability evidence (the standing eval-plane gap, cf. [`../oss-frameworks/inspect-ai.md`](../oss-frameworks/inspect-ai.md) §4). KR shape: encode a minimal dangerous-capability / behavioral-envelope check suite (drawing on the four-area methodology *and* the OAgents Appendix-C MUST-components) as Inspect `Task`s, run it against an AIDE exemplar ([Hermetic](../../exemplar-tracking/hermetic/) / [thinx-aidex](../../exemplar-tracking/thinx-aidex/)), and feed the graded result into the OrdSA evidence-up trail — turning eval-before-deployment from documented principle into executable enforcement, per [`feedback_enforcement_not_documentation`].
