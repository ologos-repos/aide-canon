# Analyst frame — IDC (MarketScapes + agentic-AI market forecasts)

> SOTA-survey finding. Shape per [`../README.md`](../README.md) → [`sota-survey/README.md`](../../sota-survey/README.md). Cadence: **medium** (annual MarketScape vendor assessments + periodic Worldwide AI IT Spending forecasts + quarterly notes — treat figures as a dated snapshot). **Most AIDE-relevant analyst frame surveyed to date: IDC's unified AI-*governance* MarketScape is the analyst evidence that the governance category the canon bets on is forming *as a market*.**

## 1. What it is

**IDC** is a tier-1 analyst house. Two of its frames are in scope here, and they bracket the canon's interest exactly — one is category-formation evidence, the other is adoption-phase timing:

- **IDC MarketScape: Worldwide Unified AI Governance Platforms (2025–2026 Vendor Assessment)** — a vendor-ranking of *unified AI governance platforms*, a category IDC describes as "established platforms designed to govern traditional AI, generative AI, and agentic AI by providing comprehensive oversight across the AI life cycle." **Microsoft was named a Leader** (anchored to Microsoft Foundry + Agent 365 as control points, integrated with Purview/Entra/Defender). The MarketScape's two scoring axes are **Capabilities** ("supplier product, go-to-market and business execution in the short term") and **Strategy** ("alignment of supplier strategies with customer requirements in a three- to five-year timeframe"), with market share shown by icon size.
- **IDC Worldwide Artificial Intelligence IT Spending Forecast** + agentic-AI press notes — the adoption-curve frame: agentic AI projected to **exceed 26% of worldwide IT spending — ~$1.3 trillion — in 2029**, with **AI spending growing 31.9% YoY (2025–2029)**, "driven by the growth of Agentic AI-enabled applications and systems to manage agentic fleets," and **>1 billion actively deployed AI-aides executing ~217 billion actions/day** by 2029.

A MarketScape is a *vendor-product ranking*, not an architecture or governance corpus. The forecast is a *market-sizing instrument*. **aide-canon is neither** — it is a governance/architecture corpus at research/exemplar stage, not a market product, so it does not appear in either frame (it is not a vendor IDC ranks). The interest is what IDC's frames *signal about the canon's bet*, not a head-to-head placement.

**AIDE-named-in-analyst-vocab signal (honest — not yet).** Per the analyst-frames mandate to track whether **AIDE / OrdSA / OAgents / MxM** appear in analyst vocabulary: **none appear in IDC's frames.** IDC's vocabulary is "unified AI governance," "AI life cycle," "agentic AI," "AI agents." There is **no** ordinal-authority, behavioral-envelope, AI-aide, or MxM construct in the analyst conversation. The honest read is *not yet* — the canon is below the analyst horizon. What *has* arrived in analyst vocabulary is the **category** (AI governance as a named, ranked market) — which is the signal that matters (§4), even though the canon's specific constructs are absent. Flag both collisions on every read.)

## 4. Relationship + synthesis

**Not a vendor, not ranked — a market-timing + category-formation signal at a coarser altitude than the canon.** This is the load-bearing finding, and it is the inverse of the vendor-stack entries: where a vendor entry is *per-axis ahead/behind/in-flight*, IDC is an *analyst lens* that the canon reads for two distinct signals:

- **Category-formation signal (validates the bet).** A *unified AI-governance MarketScape exists at all* — with a named Leader and Capabilities/Strategy scoring — is independent third-party evidence that **AI governance has formed as a market**, not merely a research concern. That is precisely the bet aide-canon places: that governance becomes the decisive layer. IDC's frame says the market agrees governance matters and is buying it. **The canon's category is real and forming** — the strongest external corroboration in the survey so far.
- **Altitude-divergence (the canon is more precise).** *What* the MarketScape rewards is **org-level, model/agent-lifecycle governance shipped as a suite** — Microsoft Leader on Foundry + Agent 365 (access, identity, policy, life-cycle monitoring; cross-ref [`../vendor-stacks/microsoft.md`](../vendor-stacks/microsoft.md)); the same lifecycle/compliance tier IBM's watsonx.governance contests (cross-ref [`../vendor-stacks/ibm.md`](../vendor-stacks/ibm.md)). The canon's altitude is **per-action behavioral envelope (OAgents) + ordinal authority (OrdSA O0–O6 authority-down/evidence-up)** — governing *what an AI-aide is permitted to do, per action, at a given authority altitude*, distinct from *whether a model/agent's lifecycle is compliant and policy-bound*. **IDC rewards governance-of-lifecycle-and-policy; the canon specifies governance-of-behavior-and-authority.** The canon's vocabulary is more precise; IDC's market is more shipped.

**The synthesis:** IDC validates the *premise* (governance is a market) without naming the canon's *altitude* (per-action authority). The MarketScape rewards the org-governance suites the canon's vendor entries already classify as *AIDE-behind on operationalized governance* — so the canon must read IDC honestly: **the market has arrived at "govern AI"; it has not yet arrived at "govern behavior + authority per action."** The canon's distinctive is the unrewarded altitude, not the rewarded one — and the spending forecast (26% of IT spend / $1.3T / >1B deployed AI-aides by 2029) times *when* that altitude becomes load-bearing: at billion-aide, 217-billion-actions/day scale, per-action envelope + ordinal authority stop being research refinements and become operational necessities. **The forecast is the canon's adoption-phase clock.** Same canon-spec ↔ market-substrate relationship the survey documents throughout: the canon is the layer the market will need *next*, evidenced by the market it is buying *now*.

## 5. Objective implication

Three Doerr-style Objective shapes follow:

1. **Defend-and-extend (ride the category, hold the altitude).** Use IDC's unified-AI-governance MarketScape as external proof that the governance category is real, while stating the canon's differentiation precisely against what the MarketScape rewards: **per-action behavioral-envelope + ordinal-authority governance** vs the org-level/lifecycle/policy governance IDC scores (Microsoft Leader, IBM watsonx.governance). KR shape: a published "where-the-canon-sits-relative-to-the-IDC-governance-category" note that *concedes* the shipped lifecycle/policy tier to the named vendors and names the per-action authority altitude as the unrewarded, canon-distinctive ground.
2. **Catch-up (productize toward analyst legibility).** The canon has no MarketScape coordinate because it is pre-product and analyst-invisible — *not yet* in IDC's vocabulary. KR shape: mature an exemplar (Hermetic / thinx-aidex / aeon-deployed) to the point its governance claims are demonstrable in analyst-legible terms (life-cycle oversight + compliance-mappable evidence, the dimensions IDC actually scores), so the canon's altitude becomes *visible from* the analyst frame rather than below its horizon.
3. **Converge-or-differentiate (time to the adoption clock).** Treat the IDC forecast (26% of IT spend / $1.3T / >1B deployed AI-aides / ~217B actions/day by 2029) as the canon's adoption-phase timing signal. KR shape: sequence canon enforcement maturity (envelope + ordinal-authority gates moving from spec to executable) against the forecast curve, so per-action governance is shipped *before* the billion-aide scale at which it becomes non-optional.
